You don't need 20% down to buy a home in California. I hear this every week from first-time buyers: "We're saving up for 20%. Almost there." Here's what nobody told them: → FHA loan: 3.5% down (credit score 580+) → Conventional: as low as 3% down → VA loan: 0% down for veterans → CalHFA MyHome: 3-3.5% of the purchase price as a deferred loan — no monthly payments on it On a $590,000 home in the Inland Empire (that's the current median in Riverside County), 3.5% down = $20,650. Not $118,000. That's the difference between buying this year and waiting three more. Yes, you'll pay PMI. Yes, your monthly will be higher than with 20% down. But you'll be building equity instead of paying someone else's mortgage through rent. The 20% rule keeps good buyers on the sidelines longer than they need to be. What questions do you have about down payments? Drop them below — I'll answer every one. #FirstTimeHomeBuyer #CaliforniaRealEstate #InlandEmpire #DownPayment #BilingualAgent

Controversial
Your landlord just raised your rent again. Here's what nobody tells you: Every rent payment builds someone's wealth. Just not yours. Your $2,800/month rent is covering your landlord's mortgage, property taxes, insurance, AND profit margin. They borrowed money to buy that building. YOU are paying it off for them. And when they sell it in 10 years for double what they paid — you get nothing. Now compare: A $570,000 mortgage at 6.7% = ~$3,700/month (principal + interest). Add taxes, insurance, and PMI = roughly $4,400-$4,800/month. More than rent? Yes. But here's the difference: → Every payment chips away at YOUR loan, not someone else's. → In 5 years, you could have $40,000-$60,000 in equity. → In 10 years, you might refinance at a lower rate and cut that payment. → Your mortgage stays fixed. Rent goes up every year. I'm not saying renting is always bad. I'm saying: understand what you're choosing. If you CAN buy and you're choosing not to — make sure it's a real decision, not just fear. Would you rather pay $2,800 to build your landlord's equity or $4,500 to build your own? #RealEstate #RentVsBuy #FirstTimeHomeBuyer #WealthBuilding #CaliforniaHousing

Education Myth-Bust
You need a 750 credit score to buy a home. FALSE. Here's what you actually need: → FHA loan: minimum 580 for 3.5% down. → FHA with 10% down: credit score as low as 500. → Conventional loan: usually 620+. → VA loan: no official minimum (lenders typically want 580-620). A 750 score gets you the best rates — yes. But a 620 score gets you IN THE DOOR.
