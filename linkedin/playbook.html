The honest picture

Where you are right now

Before fixing anything, let's name what's working and what's not.

Problem

Your leads depend on Olaf's network

Right now, most clients come through Olaf's 35+ years of connections. That works — but it means YOUR name, YOUR authority, and YOUR pipeline don't exist independently. If Olaf stops generating leads, the business slows down. LinkedIn is where you build your own reputation.

Problem

Nobody knows who you are yet on LinkedIn

You just started posting last week. That means you have near-zero visibility. The algorithm doesn't know what to do with your content yet. Your connections are probably small. This isn't a weakness — it's just the starting line. But it means everything you do in the next 8 weeks matters more than it will later.

Strength

You have a genuinely powerful story

Bilingual. Husband-wife team. 40+ combined years. Three major markets. And — the part most agents would kill for — you actually understand what it's like to navigate a huge financial decision when you don't speak the dominant language. That's not a bullet point. That's a reason someone picks up the phone.

Strength

First-time buyers are the ideal LinkedIn audience

First-time buyers are anxious, full of questions, and searching for information before they ever talk to an agent. They Google. They scroll. They read. If you show up with clear, helpful answers — you become the trusted name before they even need you.

Semana 1 · Tu cliente

Your Ideal Client Profile (ICP)

The 30X course asks you to define this precisely. Here it is for your real estate business.

Who exactly are you talking to

  • Age: 28-40 years old
  • Situation: Renting, tired of it, ready to buy but scared/confused
  • Language: Bilingual families where one or both partners are more comfortable in Spanish — OR English-speaking first-timers in IE/LA/OC
  • Income: Combined household $80K-$150K (they CAN qualify but don't know it yet)
  • Geography: Inland Empire (most affordable), with options in LA and OC
  • Biggest fear: "I can't afford it" / "I don't understand the process" / "I'll make a mistake"
  • What they need from you: Someone who explains everything clearly, in their language, without judgment, and makes them feel confident enough to act

The insight that changes everything: First-time buyers don't search for an agent first. They search for answers: "How much do I need for a down payment?" "Can I buy with a 620 credit score?" "What does escrow mean?" If YOU are the person answering those questions on LinkedIn — you're already their agent before they know they need one.

Semana 1 · Tu perfil como vitrina

Profile audit: what to fix

Your profile isn't a resume. It's a storefront. Every line should answer: "Why should a first-time buyer trust this person?"

Rewrite

Headline

Current: Real Estate Agent | Buyers & Sellers | EN/ES | Inland Empire · LA · Orange County

Problem: It describes what you are, not what you DO for people. Every agent says "Buyers & Sellers." It doesn't give a first-time buyer a reason to click.

Rewrite to:

I help first-time buyers go from renting to owning — in English or Spanish | Inland Empire · LA · OC

Why this works: It names the person (first-time buyer), names the transformation (renting → owning), and highlights the bilingual edge — all in one line. Someone scrolling sees themselves in your headline.

Rewrite

About section

Your About section should read like a conversation, not a bio. Structure it in this order:

  • Line 1-2: Hook — name the pain. "Buying your first home feels overwhelming. Especially when the process isn't explained in your language."
  • Lines 3-5: What you do. "I walk first-time buyers through every step — from pre-approval to keys in hand. In English or Spanish, whatever feels right."
  • Lines 6-8: Why you. "My husband Olaf and I are a husband-wife team with 40+ combined years in real estate. He taught me this business. Together we cover the Inland Empire, LA County, and Orange County."
  • Lines 9-10: The personal note. "I know what it's like to navigate something huge when it's not in your first language. That's exactly why I do this."
  • Last line: CTA. "If you're thinking about buying your first home, send me a message. No pressure, just answers."
Add

Banner image

Your banner (the big horizontal photo behind your headshot) is probably the default LinkedIn blue. Replace it with a photo of you and Olaf at a property, at a closing, or with keys. Add text overlay: "First home? We've got you. EN/ES" — use Canva, takes 5 minutes.

Add

Featured section

Pin 2-3 items to your Featured section:

  • Your best LinkedIn post (once you have one with engagement)
  • A "First-Time Buyer Checklist" (even a simple Canva PDF)
  • A link to a Google Form: "Not sure if you can buy? Answer 5 questions and I'll tell you where you stand" — this becomes a lead magnet
Keep

Experience & Skills

Manna Realty experience + your skill list are solid. Keep them. Add a short description under Manna Realty that says what you actually do day-to-day for clients (not job duties — outcomes).

Semana 2-3 · Tu contenido

Your 4 content pillars

Everything you post should fall into one of these. This keeps your feed focused and your audience clear on what you're about.

🔑

First-Time Buyer Education

Break down the homebuying process in plain language. Down payments, credit scores, pre-approval, closing costs, what to expect. The stuff nobody explains clearly.

📊

Local Market Reality

Real numbers from IE, LA, OC. Median prices, inventory, rate updates. Not just data — what it MEANS for a buyer right now. "Here's what $630K buys you in the Inland Empire."

🤝

The Team / Behind the Scenes

You and Olaf at work. A closing photo. A funny moment at a showing. A story about a client win (with permission). People buy from people they know.

💬

Myth-Busting / Common Questions

"You DON'T need 20% down." "You CAN buy with student loans." "Here's when renting makes more sense than buying." Honest answers build trust.

The ratio: 3 out of 4 posts should educate or help. Only 1 in 4 should be about you, your team, or a listing. People follow educators, not salespeople.

What "engage" means on Tue/Thu

  • Comment on 5 posts from people in your target market (other agents, lenders, title reps, local businesses in IE/LA/OC)
  • Don't just say "Great post!" — add a real thought, a stat, or a follow-up question
  • This puts your face and name in front of THEIR audience. It's free distribution.
  • Find these people: search "first-time homebuyer" or "Inland Empire real estate" on LinkedIn, follow the active posters, and engage consistently

Post formatting rules that get more reach

  • Hook in line 1. The first line is everything — it determines if people click "see more." Lead with a question, a surprising stat, or a bold statement.
  • Short paragraphs. 1-2 sentences max per paragraph. White space is your friend on LinkedIn.
  • End with a question. "What's stopping you from buying?" / "Did you know this?" / "What would you add?" — this drives comments, which drives reach.
  • 3-5 hashtags max. At the bottom. Use: #FirstTimeHomeBuyer #InlandEmpireRealEstate #RealEstate #BilingualAgent #SoCalHomes
  • Photos of YOU outperform graphics. A selfie at a property beats a Canva carousel every time. Real > polished.

Semana 4-5 · Prospección

Finding your first clients on LinkedIn

Posting builds authority. Prospecting builds pipeline. You need both.

Who to connect with (and how)

  • Lenders / mortgage brokers in IE, LA, OC — they have pre-approved buyers looking for agents. Comment on their posts. Build the relationship FIRST, then ask for coffee or a Zoom.
  • First-time buyer program coordinators — HUD counselors, CalHFA reps, nonprofit homebuyer education orgs. They refer people who are ready.
  • HR managers at large IE employers — Amazon, logistics companies, hospitals. Their employees relocate and need housing.
  • Other agents in adjacent markets — an LA agent who gets a client that needs IE pricing will refer to someone they trust. Be that someone.
  • Title/escrow officers — they know everyone. Engage with their content.

Connection request message (keep it short)

"Hi [Name] — I saw your post about [topic]. I'm a bilingual agent in the IE/LA/OC area, focused on helping first-time buyers. Would love to connect and see how we might help each other's clients."

Never pitch in the first message. Build the connection. Let your content do the selling.

The 30X PACS framework applies here: don't jump to selling. The sequence is: Profile (your storefront) → Authority (content) → Community (network) → Sales (conversations). You're still in P and A. Build those before you push for S.

The real talk

What most agents get wrong

They post listings. Nobody cares about your listings on LinkedIn.

Your MLS feed is not content. A listing post gets 3 likes from other agents. An education post about "5 things your lender won't tell you" gets saved, shared, and remembered by actual buyers. Post value, not inventory.

They post once and expect leads. It takes 90 days.

Weeks 1-4: you're invisible. You're building the habit. Weeks 5-8: the algorithm starts showing you to more people. Months 3-6: people start recognizing your name and reaching out. Don't quit in week 3 because "it's not working." It IS working — you just can't see it yet.

They never tell their personal story.

Your bilingual, husband-wife, learned-it-from-scratch story is worth more than any market update. People don't choose agents because of data. They choose agents because "I feel like she gets me." Share your story early and often.
