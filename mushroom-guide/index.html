Chanterelle

Cantharellus cibarius

Key ID: Golden funnel shape, false gills (forked ridges, not blades), fruity apricot smell. Grows from soil, never wood.

What It Looks Like

  • Cap: Golden to egg-yolk yellow, 2–5 in wide, wavy funnel with irregular edges
  • Underside: False gills — forked ridges running down the stem (NOT blade-like)
  • Stem: Solid, same golden color, tapers toward base
  • Flesh: White to pale yellow when cut
  • Smell: Fruity, like apricots

Where & When

Forest floors near oaks, conifers, hardwoods. Scattered or small groups. Summer–early fall.

Watch Out: Jack O'Lantern (Toxic #10)

If You Find One

Cook thoroughly in butter or oil. Never eat raw. Meaty, peppery, slightly fruity — one of the best wild mushrooms in the world.

Edible Mar–May

Morel

Morchella esculenta

Key ID: Honeycomb cap with deep pits. Completely hollow from top to bottom when sliced. Cone-shaped, tan to dark brown.

What It Looks Like

  • Cap: Honeycomb surface with deep pits and ridges, tan to dark brown, cone-shaped
  • Inside: Completely hollow top to bottom — the #1 ID feature
  • Stem: White to cream, also hollow, slightly granular
  • Size: 2–6 inches tall

Where & When

Near dead/dying elms, ash, apple trees. Post-wildfire areas are goldmines. Spring (Mar–May), one of the first mushrooms of the year.

Watch Out: False Morel (Deadly)

False morels have brain-like wrinkled caps (not clean honeycomb) and are NOT hollow inside — they have cottony, chambered flesh. Slice it in half: hollow = true morel, chambered = walk away.

If You Find One

Soak in salt water 30 min to remove insects. Cook thoroughly. Nutty, earthy, considered a delicacy.

Edible May–Nov

Chicken of the Woods

Laetiporus sulphureus

Key ID: Bright orange/yellow shelf fungus in overlapping layers on tree trunks. Sulfur-yellow underside with tiny pores.

What It Looks Like

  • Shape: Large shelf/bracket in overlapping layers on trunks or logs
  • Top: Bright orange to salmon
  • Underside: Sulfur-yellow, tiny pores (no gills)
  • Flesh: Thick, white, firm when young — chalky when old

Where & When

On dead or living hardwoods, especially oaks. Late spring–fall. Always on wood, never from ground.

Important Notes

Avoid specimens on conifers, eucalyptus, or yew — can cause GI distress. Only eat young, vibrant ones.

If You Find One

Texture like actual chicken breast. Slice tender outer edges, sauté with garlic.

🔒
Returns to the same oak every single year.
Edible Sep–Nov

Hen of the Woods (Maitake)

Grifola frondosa

Key ID: Large rosette of gray-brown overlapping fans at tree bases (especially oaks). White pores underneath. Can grow 10–50+ lbs.

What It Looks Like

  • Shape: Ruffled rosette of overlapping fan-shaped caps from one base
  • Color: Gray-brown on top, white underneath
  • Underside: Tiny white pores (no gills)
  • Returns to the same spot year after year

Where & When

Base of living or dead hardwoods, especially oaks. Fall (Sep–Nov).

Watch Out: Black-Staining Polypore

Stains black when bruised or cut. Hen of the woods does not.

If You Find One

Tear into pieces, sauté until crispy on edges. Rich umami flavor.

🔒
No dangerous look-alikes exist. A beginner's dream.
Edible Aug–Nov

Lion's Mane

Hericium erinaceus

Key ID: White pom-pom with cascading icicle-like spines. No cap, no stem, no gills. Virtually no dangerous look-alikes.

What It Looks Like

  • Shape: Single white globe with long cascading spines
  • Color: Pure white when young, yellowing with age
  • Unique: No cap, no stem, no gills — nothing else looks like it

Where & When

On dead/dying hardwoods (beech, oak, maple). Late summer–fall.

If You Find One

Slice into thick "steaks" and sear in butter. Texture like crab or lobster.

🔒
Grows year-round. Most versatile wild mushroom.
Edible Year-round

Oyster Mushroom

Pleurotus ostreatus

Key ID: Fan/shell-shaped, white to gray, grows in shelf-like clusters on dead hardwood. Short off-center stem. Mild anise smell.

What It Looks Like

  • Cap: Fan or oyster-shell shaped, 2–8 in wide, smooth
  • Color: White, cream, gray, or tan
  • Gills: White, running down a short stubby stem

Where & When

Dead/dying hardwoods. Spring & fall primarily, but year-round in mild climates.

Watch Out: Angel Wings

Angel wings are pure white, paper-thin, on conifer wood. Oyster mushrooms are thicker, on hardwood.

If You Find One

One of the most versatile wild mushrooms. Sauté, stir-fry, add to soups. Mild, slightly sweet.

🔒
Worth $50/lb dried. The king of mushrooms.
Edible Jul–Oct

Porcini (King Bolete)

Boletus edulis

Key ID: Brown convex cap, spongy pores underneath (NOT gills), thick bulbous white stem with fine white netting. Flesh does NOT change color.

What It Looks Like

  • Cap: Smooth, convex, brown to reddish-brown, 3–10 in
  • Underside: Spongy pore surface — white young, yellow-green with age
  • Stem: Thick, bulbous, white netting on upper portion

Where & When

Under conifers and some hardwoods, in mountainous/northern forests. Summer–fall.

Watch Out: Bitter Bolete

Looks identical but tastes intensely bitter. Pore surface turns pinkish with age. Not toxic, just ruins the dish.

If You Find One

One of the most sought-after mushrooms worldwide. Slice and sauté fresh, or dry for risottos and pastas.

Deadly Toxic Aug–Nov

Death Cap

Amanita phalloides

Key ID: Greenish-yellow to olive cap, white gills, skirt-like ring on stem + cup-shaped volva at base (dig it up!). Smells like honey. Half a cap can kill an adult.

What It Looks Like

  • Cap: 2–6 in, greenish-yellow to olive-green, smooth, slightly sticky
  • Gills: White, closely spaced, free (not attached to stem)
  • Stem: White, 3–6 in, with a ring (annulus) near the top
  • Base: Bulbous, sitting in a cup-like sac (volva) — often buried. DIG IT UP
  • Smell: Pleasant — honey or roses. This is deceptive.

Where & When

Under oaks and hardwoods. Parks, yards, forests. Fall/winter in mild climates, late summer–fall elsewhere.

Why It Kills

  • Contains amatoxins that destroy liver and kidneys
  • Symptoms delayed 6–24 hours — organ damage may already be happening
  • Deceptive "recovery" phase before final, potentially fatal stage
  • No reliable home antidote — ER immediately
🔒
All white. All innocent-looking. All deadly.
Deadly Toxic Jun–Oct

Destroying Angel

Amanita bisporigera / A. virosa / A. ocreata

Key ID: Entirely pure white. White gills, fragile ring on stem, volva at base. Looks elegant and innocent. As deadly as the Death Cap.

What It Looks Like

  • Cap: 2–5 in, pure white, smooth, convex to flat
  • Gills: White, free from stem
  • Stem: White, tall, slender, fragile ring
  • Base: Bulbous with sac-like volva — DIG IT UP

The #1 Rule with White Mushrooms

If it's white, has gills, and grows from the ground — treat it as deadly until proven otherwise. Contains the same amatoxins as the Death Cap.

🔒
#1 reason people mistake toxic for edible.
Toxic Jul–Nov

Jack O'Lantern

Omphalotus olearius

Key ID: Bright orange, true sharp gills, grows in clusters on/near wood. Gills glow in the dark. The #1 Chanterelle impostor.

If Someone Eats a Toxic Mushroom

1-800-222-1222

US Poison Control — Call immediately. Do NOT wait for symptoms.

  1. Call now. With Death Cap/Destroying Angel, symptoms are delayed 6–24 hours — organ damage starts before you feel sick.
  2. Save a sample or take clear photos of the mushroom.
  3. Do NOT induce vomiting unless told to by Poison Control.
  4. Go to the ER. Do not wait it out at home.
  5. Note the time of ingestion and when symptoms appeared.

Trail Extras

Everything else you need for mushroom hunting

The Essentials

  • Small knife or pocket blade — cut mushrooms at the base cleanly
  • Paper bags or wax paper — never plastic (traps moisture)
  • Basket or mesh bag — keeps finds intact, drops spores as you walk
  • Your phone camera — photos of cap, underside, stem, base, habitat

Nice to Have

  • 10x hand lens — examine gill structure up close
  • Field notebook — tree species nearby, soil type, weather
  • Aluminum foil — wrap specimens individually
  • White + dark paper — spore prints on the trail
  • Wet wipes — always wash hands after handling unknowns
  • Where is it growing? Ground, wood, tree trunk?
  • What's nearby? Oak, beech, conifer, mixed forest?
  • How is it growing? Alone, scattered, dense cluster?
  • Cap: Color, shape, size, texture?
  • Underside: Gills, pores, ridges, or teeth?
  • Stem: Color, thickness, ring/skirt, cup at base?
  • Cut it open: Hollow? Solid? Color change?
  • Smell: Fruity? Earthy? Unpleasant?
  • Photos taken? Top, underside, stem, base, habitat — at least 5

Chanterelle (Rebozuelo)

Cantharellus cibarius

Clave: Embudo dorado, falsas láminas (pliegues bifurcados), olor afrutado a albaricoque. Crece del suelo, nunca de madera.

Cómo Se Ve

  • Sombrero: Dorado a amarillo yema, 5–12 cm, ondulado tipo embudo
  • Parte inferior: Falsas láminas — pliegues que bajan por el tallo
  • Tallo: Sólido, mismo color dorado
  • Olor: Afrutado, como albaricoque

Dónde y Cuándo

Pisos de bosque cerca de robles y coníferas. Verano a principios de otoño.

Morilla (Morel)

Morchella esculenta

Clave: Sombrero tipo panal con hoyos profundos. Completamente hueco al cortarlo. Forma de cono, canela a marrón oscuro.

Cómo Se Ve

  • Sombrero: Superficie de panal con hoyos y crestas
  • Interior: Completamente hueco — la clave #1
  • Tallo: Blanco a crema, también hueco

Dónde y Cuándo

Cerca de olmos y fresnos muertos. Áreas post-incendio. Primavera (Mar–May).

Cuidado: Falsa Morilla (Mortal)

Sombreros arrugados tipo cerebro. NO hueca. Córtala: hueca = verdadera.

Si Encuentras Uno

Remoja en agua con sal 30 min. Cocina completamente. Un manjar.

Pollo del Bosque

Laetiporus sulphureus

Clave: Repisa naranja/amarilla brillante en capas superpuestas. Parte inferior amarillo azufre con poros diminutos.

Cómo Se Ve

  • Arriba: Naranja brillante a salmón

En maderas duras, especialmente robles. Final de primavera a otoño.

Clave: Forma de abanico/concha, blanco a gris, en racimos sobre madera dura muerta.

Cómo Se Ve

Hongo de la Muerte (Death Cap)

Amanita phalloides

Clave: Sombrero verde-amarillento a oliva, láminas blancas, anillo tipo falda + volva tipo copa en la base. Huele a miel. Media cápsula mata a un adulto.

Cómo Se Ve

  • Sombrero: 5–15 cm, verde-amarillento a oliva, liso
  • Láminas: Blancas, muy juntas, libres del tallo
  • Tallo: Blanco, 8–15 cm, con anillo
  • Base: Bulbosa con volva — DESENTIERRA LA BASE

Por Qué Mata

  • Amatoxinas que destruyen hígado y riñones
  • Síntomas se retrasan 6–24 horas
  • Fase engañosa de "mejoría" antes de la etapa final
  • Sin antídoto casero — urgencias inmediatamente
🔒
Todo blanco. Todo inocente. Todo mortal.
MortalJun–Oct

Ángel Destructor

Amanita bisporigera / A. virosa / A. ocreata

Clave: Todo blanco puro. Láminas blancas, anillo frágil, volva en la base. Tan letal como el Hongo de la Muerte.

Cómo Se Ve

  • Sombrero blanco puro, 5–12 cm, liso
  • Láminas blancas, libres del tallo
  • Tallo blanco, alto, anillo frágil
  • Base bulbosa con volva — DESENTIERRA

Regla #1 con Hongos Blancos

Si es blanco, tiene láminas y crece del suelo — trátalo como mortal.

Si Alguien Come un Hongo Tóxico

1-800-222-1222

Control de Envenenamientos de EE.UU. — Llama inmediatamente. NO esperes síntomas.

  1. Llama ahora. Con el Hongo de la Muerte, los síntomas se retrasan 6–24 horas.
  2. Guarda una muestra o toma fotos claras.
  3. NO provoques el vómito a menos que te lo indique un médico.
  4. Ve a urgencias. No intentes esperar en casa.
  5. Anota la hora de ingestión y cuándo aparecieron los síntomas.

Extras para el Sendero

Todo lo que necesitas para buscar hongos

Lo Esencial

  • Cuchillo pequeño — corta hongos limpiamente en la base
  • Bolsas de papel — nunca plástico
  • Canasta o bolsa de malla — mantiene los hallazgos intactos
  • Tu teléfono (cámara) — fotos del sombrero, abajo, tallo, base

Bueno Tener

  • Lupa 10x — examina láminas y poros de cerca
  • Libreta de campo — árboles cercanos, tipo de suelo
  • Papel aluminio — envuelve ejemplares
  • Papel blanco + oscuro — para huellas de esporas
  • ¿Dónde crece? Suelo, madera, tronco?
  • ¿Qué hay cerca? Roble, haya, conífera?
  • ¿Cómo crece? Solo, disperso, racimo?
  • Sombrero: Color, forma, tamaño, textura?
  • Parte inferior: Láminas, poros, pliegues, espinas?
  • Tallo: Color, grosor, anillo, copa en la base?
  • Córtalo: Hueco? Sólido? Cambia de color?
  • Olor: Afrutado? Terroso? Desagradable?
  • ¿Fotos tomadas? Al menos 5 ángulos
