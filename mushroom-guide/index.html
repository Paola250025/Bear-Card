Edible Jun–Oct

Chanterelle

Cantharellus cibarius

Key ID: Golden funnel shape, false gills (forked ridges, not blades), fruity apricot smell. Grows from soil, never wood.

What It Looks Like

  • Cap: Golden to egg-yolk yellow, 2–5 in wide, wavy funnel with irregular edges
  • Underside: False gills — forked ridges running down the stem (NOT blade-like)
  • Stem: Solid, same golden color, tapers toward base
  • Flesh: White to pale yellow when cut
  • Smell: Fruity, like apricots

Where & When

Forest floors near oaks, conifers, hardwoods. Scattered or small groups. Summer–early fall.

Watch Out: Jack O'Lantern (Toxic #10)

FeatureChanterelleJack O'Lantern
GillsFalse gills (ridges)True, sharp gills
GrowthFrom soil, scatteredClusters on/near wood
InsidePale/white fleshOrange throughout
GlowNoYes (bioluminescent)

If You Find One

Cook thoroughly in butter or oil. Never eat raw. Meaty, peppery, slightly fruity — one of the best wild mushrooms in the world.

Edible Mar–May

Morel

Morchella esculenta

Key ID: Honeycomb cap with deep pits. Completely hollow from top to bottom when sliced. Cone-shaped, tan to dark brown.

What It Looks Like

  • Cap: Honeycomb surface with deep pits and ridges, tan to dark brown, cone-shaped
  • Inside: Completely hollow top to bottom — the #1 ID feature
  • Stem: White to cream, also hollow, slightly granular
  • Size: 2–6 inches tall

Where & When

Near dead/dying elms, ash, apple trees. Post-wildfire areas are goldmines. Spring (Mar–May), one of the first mushrooms of the year.

Watch Out: False Morel (Deadly)

False morels have brain-like wrinkled caps (not clean honeycomb) and are NOT hollow inside — they have cottony, chambered flesh. Slice it in half: hollow = true morel, chambered = walk away.

If You Find One

Soak in salt water 30 min to remove insects. Cook thoroughly. Nutty, earthy, considered a delicacy.

Edible May–Nov

Chicken of the Woods

Laetiporus sulphureus

Key ID: Bright orange/yellow shelf fungus in overlapping layers on tree trunks. Sulfur-yellow underside with tiny pores. Can be huge (2+ ft clusters).

What It Looks Like

  • Shape: Large shelf/bracket in overlapping layers on trunks or logs
  • Top: Bright orange to salmon
  • Underside: Sulfur-yellow, tiny pores (no gills)
  • Flesh: Thick, white, firm when young — chalky when old

Where & When

On dead or living hardwoods, especially oaks. Late spring–fall. Always on wood, never from ground.

Important Notes

Avoid specimens on conifers, eucalyptus, or yew — can cause GI distress. Only eat young, vibrant ones (old ones turn chalky and crumbly).

If You Find One

Texture like actual chicken breast. Slice tender outer edges, sauté with garlic. Skip the tough base.

Edible Sep–Nov

Hen of the Woods (Maitake)

Grifola frondosa

Key ID: Large rosette of gray-brown overlapping fans at tree bases (especially oaks). White pores underneath. Can grow 10–50+ lbs.

What It Looks Like

  • Shape: Ruffled rosette of overlapping fan-shaped caps from one base
  • Color: Gray-brown on top, white underneath
  • Underside: Tiny white pores (no gills)
  • Returns to the same spot year after year

Where & When

Base of living or dead hardwoods, especially oaks. Fall (Sep–Nov).

Watch Out: Black-Staining Polypore

Similar look but stains black when bruised or cut. Hen of the woods does not. Not dangerous, just not as tasty.

If You Find One

Prized in Japanese cuisine. Tear into pieces, sauté until crispy on edges. Rich umami flavor. Dries and freezes well.

Edible Aug–Nov

Lion's Mane

Hericium erinaceus

Key ID: White pom-pom with cascading icicle-like spines. No cap, no stem, no gills. Virtually no dangerous look-alikes.

What It Looks Like

  • Shape: Single white globe with long cascading spines
  • Color: Pure white when young, yellowing with age
  • Unique: No cap, no stem, no gills — nothing else looks like it
  • Size: 4–12 inches across

Where & When

On dead/dying hardwoods (beech, oak, maple). Often high up on standing dead trees. Late summer–fall.

If You Find One

Slice into thick "steaks" and sear in butter until golden. Texture like crab or lobster. Also valued for cognitive health benefits.

Edible Year-round

Oyster Mushroom

Pleurotus ostreatus

Key ID: Fan/shell-shaped, white to gray, grows in shelf-like clusters on dead hardwood. Short off-center stem. Mild anise smell.

What It Looks Like

  • Cap: Fan or oyster-shell shaped, 2–8 in wide, smooth
  • Color: White, cream, gray, or tan
  • Gills: White, running down a short stubby stem
  • Grows on: Dead hardwood logs, stumps, standing dead trees

Where & When

Dead/dying hardwoods. Loves fallen logs in damp forests. Spring & fall primarily, but year-round in mild climates — even winter.

Watch Out: Angel Wings

Angel wings are pure white, paper-thin, and grow on conifer wood. Oyster mushrooms are thicker, fleshier, on hardwood. Stick to hardwood specimens.

If You Find One

One of the most versatile wild mushrooms. Sauté, stir-fry, bread and fry, add to soups. Mild, slightly sweet.

Edible Jul–Oct

Porcini (King Bolete)

Boletus edulis

Key ID: Brown convex cap, spongy pores underneath (NOT gills), thick bulbous white stem with fine white netting. Flesh does NOT change color when cut.

What It Looks Like

  • Cap: Smooth, convex, brown to reddish-brown, 3–10 in, slightly sticky when wet
  • Underside: Spongy pore surface — white young, yellow-green with age
  • Stem: Thick, bulbous, white netting (reticulation) on upper portion
  • Size: 4–10 in tall, sturdy and hefty

Where & When

Under conifers (spruce, pine, fir) and some hardwoods, in mountainous/northern forests. Summer–fall.

Watch Out: Bitter Bolete

Looks nearly identical but tastes intensely bitter. Coarser/darker stem netting. Pore surface turns pinkish with age (porcini stays yellow-green). Not toxic, just ruins the dish.

If You Find One

One of the most sought-after culinary mushrooms worldwide. Slice and sauté fresh, or dry for incredible concentrated flavor in risottos and pastas. Stem is edible too.

Deadly Toxic Aug–Nov

Death Cap

Amanita phalloides

Key ID: Greenish-yellow to olive cap, white gills, skirt-like ring on stem + cup-shaped volva at base (dig it up!). Smells like honey. Half a cap can kill an adult.

What It Looks Like

  • Cap: 2–6 in, greenish-yellow to olive-green, smooth, slightly sticky
  • Gills: White, closely spaced, free (not attached to stem)
  • Stem: White, 3–6 in, with a ring (annulus) near the top
  • Base: Bulbous, sitting in a cup-like sac (volva) — often buried. DIG IT UP
  • Smell: Pleasant — honey or roses. This is deceptive.

Where & When

Under oaks and hardwoods. Parks, yards, forests. Originally European, now widespread in North America. Fall/winter in mild climates, late summer–fall elsewhere.

Why It Kills

  • Contains amatoxins that destroy liver and kidneys
  • Symptoms delayed 6–24 hours — organ damage may already be happening
  • Deceptive "recovery" phase before final, potentially fatal stage
  • No reliable home antidote — ER immediately
Deadly Toxic Jun–Oct

Destroying Angel

Amanita bisporigera / A. virosa / A. ocreata

Key ID: Entirely pure white. White gills, fragile ring on stem, volva at base (dig it up!). Looks elegant and innocent. As deadly as the Death Cap.

What It Looks Like

  • Cap: 2–5 in, pure white, smooth, convex to flat
  • Gills: White, free from stem
  • Stem: White, tall, slender, fragile ring (may disappear with age)
  • Base: Bulbous with sac-like volva — often underground. DIG IT UP
  • Entire mushroom: All white. Looks harmless. It's not.

Where & When

Near oaks, pines, mixed forests. Also lawns and gardens near trees. Summer–fall.

The #1 Rule with White Mushrooms

If it's white, has gills, and grows from the ground — treat it as deadly until proven otherwise. This single rule could save your life. Contains the same amatoxins as the Death Cap.

Toxic Jul–Nov

Jack O'Lantern

Omphalotus olearius

Key ID: Bright orange, true sharp gills (not ridges), grows in clusters on/near wood. Gills glow in the dark. The #1 Chanterelle impostor.

What It Looks Like

  • Cap: 2–8 in, bright orange, smooth, convex to funnel-shaped
  • Gills: True, sharp blade-like gills — orange, closely spaced. Glow faintly green in complete darkness
  • Stem: Orange, solid, central
  • Always on wood: Stumps, buried roots, tree bases
  • Growth: Dense clusters (multiple from same point)

Chanterelle vs. Jack O'Lantern

FeatureChanterelle (Safe)Jack O'Lantern (Toxic)
GillsFalse gills (ridges)True, sharp gills
GrowthFrom soil, scatteredClusters on/near wood
InsidePale/white fleshOrange throughout
GlowNoYes
SmellFruity, apricotUnpleasant

If Someone Eats One

Severe cramps, vomiting, diarrhea within 1–2 hours. Extremely unpleasant but rarely fatal. Seek medical attention.

If Someone Eats a Toxic Mushroom

1-800-222-1222

US Poison Control — Call immediately. Do NOT wait for symptoms.

  1. Call now. With Death Cap/Destroying Angel, symptoms are delayed 6–24 hours — organ damage starts before you feel sick.
  2. Save a sample or take clear photos of the mushroom.
  3. Do NOT induce vomiting unless told to by Poison Control.
  4. Go to the ER. Do not wait it out at home.
  5. Note the time of ingestion and when symptoms appeared.

Trail Extras

Everything else you need for mushroom hunting

The Essentials

  • Small knife or pocket blade — cut mushrooms at the base cleanly
  • Paper bags or wax paper — never plastic (traps moisture, destroys specimens)
  • Basket or mesh bag — keeps finds intact, drops spores as you walk
  • Your phone camera — photos of cap, underside, stem, base, habitat. Non-negotiable

Nice to Have

  • 10x hand lens — examine gill structure and pore surfaces up close
  • Field notebook / notes app — tree species nearby, soil type, weather
  • Aluminum foil — wrap specimens individually
  • White + dark paper — spore prints on the trail
  • Wet wipes — always wash hands after handling unknowns
  • Where is it growing? Ground, wood, tree trunk, buried wood?
  • What's nearby? Oak, beech, conifer, mixed forest?
  • How is it growing? Alone, scattered, dense cluster?
  • Cap: Color, shape, size, texture (smooth, sticky, scaly)?
  • Underside: Gills, pores, ridges, or teeth/spines?
  • Stem: Color, thickness, ring/skirt, cup at base?
  • Cut it open: Hollow? Solid? Does flesh change color?
  • Smell: Fruity? Earthy? Unpleasant? Nothing?
  • Photos taken? Top, underside, stem, base, habitat — at least 5
  • Spore print started? White + dark paper, gill-side down, 4–8 hours

Comestible Jun–Oct

Chanterelle (Rebozuelo)

Cantharellus cibarius

Clave: Embudo dorado, falsas laminas (pliegues bifurcados, no cuchillas), olor afrutado a albaricoque. Crece del suelo, nunca de madera.

Como Se Ve

  • Sombrero: Dorado a amarillo yema, 5–12 cm, ondulado y con forma de embudo
  • Parte inferior: Falsas laminas — pliegues bifurcados que bajan por el tallo
  • Tallo: Solido, mismo color dorado, se estrecha hacia la base
  • Carne: Blanca a amarillo palido al cortarlo
  • Olor: Afrutado, como albaricoque

Donde y Cuando

Pisos de bosque cerca de robles y coniferas. Disperso o en grupos pequenos. Verano a principios de otono.

Cuidado con: Jack O'Lantern (Toxico #10)

CrecimientoDel suelo, dispersoRacimos sobre madera
InteriorCarne palidaNaranja por completo
BrilloNoSi (bioluminiscente)

Si Encuentras Uno

Cocina bien en mantequilla o aceite. Nunca crudo. Carnoso, picante, afrutado — uno de los mejores hongos silvestres del mundo.

Comestible Mar–May

Morilla (Morel)

Morchella esculenta

Clave: Sombrero tipo panal con hoyos profundos. Completamente hueco al cortarlo por la mitad. Forma de cono, canela a marron oscuro.

Como Se Ve

  • Sombrero: Superficie de panal con hoyos y crestas, canela a marron oscuro
  • Interior: Completamente hueco — la clave #1
  • Tallo: Blanco a crema, tambien hueco

Donde y Cuando

Cerca de olmos, fresnos, manzanos muertos. Areas post-incendio. Primavera (Mar–May).

Cuidado: Falsa Morilla (Mortal)

Sombreros arrugados tipo cerebro (no panal limpio). NO hueca — carne algodonosa. Cortala: hueca = verdadera, compartimentada = alejate.

Si Encuentras Uno

Remoja en agua con sal 30 min. Cocina completamente. Terrosa, sabor a nuez, un manjar.

Comestible May–Nov

Pollo del Bosque

Laetiporus sulphureus

Clave: Repisa naranja/amarilla brillante en capas superpuestas sobre troncos. Parte inferior amarillo azufre con poros diminutos. Puede ser enorme (60+ cm).

Como Se Ve

  • Forma: Repisas superpuestas en troncos o trozos
  • Arriba: Naranja brillante a salmon
  • Abajo: Amarillo azufre, poros diminutos (sin laminas)
  • Carne: Gruesa, blanca, firme cuando joven

Donde y Cuando

En maderas duras, especialmente robles. Final de primavera a otono. Siempre sobre madera.

Nota Importante

Evita los que crecen en coniferas, eucalipto o tejo. Solo come los jovenes y vibrantes.

Si Encuentras Uno

Textura similar a pechuga de pollo. Corta los bordes tiernos, saltea con ajo.

Comestible Sep–Nov

Gallina del Bosque (Maitake)

Grifola frondosa

Clave: Roseta de abanicos gris-marron superpuestos al pie de arboles (robles). Poros blancos debajo. Puede pesar 5–25+ kg.

Como Se Ve

  • Forma: Roseta de abanicos superpuestos desde una sola base
  • Color: Gris-marron arriba, blanco abajo
  • Regresa al mismo lugar cada ano

Donde y Cuando

Base de maderas duras, especialmente robles. Otono (Sep–Nov).

Cuidado: Poliporo de Mancha Negra

Se mancha de negro al golpear o cortar. La gallina del bosque no.

Si Encuentras Uno

Desgarralo en trozos, saltea hasta crujiente. Sabor umami increible.

Comestible Ago–Nov

Melena de Leon

Hericium erinaceus

Clave: Pompon blanco con espinas colgantes tipo carabano. Sin sombrero, sin tallo, sin laminas. Practicamente sin dobles toxicos.

Como Se Ve

  • Forma: Bola blanca con espinas largas y colgantes
  • Color: Blanco puro cuando joven, se amarillenta con la edad
  • Unico: Nada se le parece

Donde y Cuando

En maderas duras muertas (haya, roble, arce). Final de verano a otono.

Si Encuentras Uno

Corta en "filetes" y sella en mantequilla. Textura como cangrejo o langosta.

Comestible Todo el ano

Hongo Ostra

Pleurotus ostreatus

Clave: Forma de abanico/concha, blanco a gris, en racimos tipo repisa sobre madera dura muerta. Tallo corto descentrado. Olor a anis.

Como Se Ve

  • Sombrero: Abanico o concha, 5–20 cm, liso
  • Color: Blanco, crema, gris o canela
  • Crece en: Madera dura muerta — troncos, tocones

Donde y Cuando

Maderas duras muertas. Primavera y otono, pero todo el ano en climas templados.

Cuidado: Alas de Angel

Blanco puro, delgadas como papel, en madera de coniferas. Hongos ostra son mas gruesos, en madera dura.

Si Encuentras Uno

Versatil. Saltea, agrega a sopas, empaniza y frie. Sabor suave y dulce.

Comestible Jul–Oct

Porcini (Boleto Rey)

Boletus edulis

Clave: Sombrero marron convexo, poros esponjosos debajo (NO laminas), tallo grueso y bulboso con reticulado blanco fino. La carne NO cambia de color.

Como Se Ve

  • Sombrero: Liso, convexo, marron, 8–25 cm
  • Abajo: Poros esponjosos — blancos de joven, amarillo-verdosos con la edad
  • Tallo: Grueso, bulboso, reticulado blanco fino en la parte superior

Donde y Cuando

Bajo coniferas en bosques montanosos y del norte. Verano a otono.

Cuidado: Boleto Amargo

Se ve igual pero sabe intensamente amargo. Reticulado mas grueso y oscuro. Poros se tornan rosados. No toxico, pero arruina el platillo.

Si Encuentras Uno

Uno de los mas buscados del mundo. Saltea fresco o seca para risottos y pastas.

Mortal Ago–Nov

Hongo de la Muerte (Death Cap)

Amanita phalloides

Clave: Sombrero verde-amarillento a oliva, laminas blancas, anillo tipo falda + volva tipo copa en la base (desentierra!). Huele a miel. Media capsula mata a un adulto.

Como Se Ve

  • Sombrero: 5–15 cm, verde-amarillento a oliva, liso
  • Laminas: Blancas, muy juntas, libres del tallo
  • Tallo: Blanco, 8–15 cm, con anillo cerca de arriba
  • Base: Bulbosa con volva tipo saco — frecuentemente enterrada. DESENTIERRA LA BASE

Por Que Mata

  • Amatoxinas que destruyen higado y rinones
  • Sintomas se retrasan 6–24 horas
  • Fase enganosa de "mejoria" antes de la etapa final
  • Sin antidoto casero — urgencias inmediatamente
Mortal Jun–Oct

Angel Destructor

Amanita bisporigera / A. virosa / A. ocreata

Clave: Todo blanco puro. Laminas blancas, anillo fragil, volva en la base (desentierra!). Parece inofensivo. Tan letal como el Hongo de la Muerte.

Como Se Ve

  • Sombrero: 5–12 cm, blanco puro, liso
  • Laminas: Blancas, libres del tallo
  • Tallo: Blanco, alto, esbelto, anillo fragil
  • Base: Bulbosa con volva tipo saco — DESENTIERRA LA BASE

Regla #1 con Hongos Blancos

Si es blanco, tiene laminas y crece del suelo — tratalo como mortal hasta que se demuestre lo contrario. Contiene las mismas amatoxinas que el Hongo de la Muerte.

Toxico Jul–Nov

Jack O'Lantern (Hongo Linterna)

Omphalotus olearius

Clave: Naranja brillante, laminas verdaderas y filosas (no pliegues), crece en racimos sobre/cerca de madera. Laminas brillan en la oscuridad. El impostor #1 del Chanterelle.

Si Alguien Come Uno

Calambres severos, vomito, diarrea en 1–2 horas. Rara vez fatal. Busca atencion medica.

Si Alguien Come un Hongo Toxico

1-800-222-1222

Control de Envenenamientos de EE.UU. — Llama inmediatamente. NO esperes sintomas.

  1. Llama ahora. Con el Hongo de la Muerte/Angel Destructor, los sintomas se retrasan 6–24 horas — el dano a organos empieza antes de que te sientas mal.
  2. Guarda una muestra o toma fotos claras del hongo.
  3. NO provoques el vomito a menos que te lo indique un medico.
  4. Ve a urgencias. No intentes esperar en casa.
  5. Anota la hora de ingestion y cuando aparecieron los sintomas.

Extras para el Sendero

Todo lo que necesitas para buscar hongos

Lo Esencial

  • Cuchillo pequeno o navaja — corta hongos limpiamente en la base
  • Bolsas de papel — nunca plastico (atrapa humedad, destruye ejemplares)
  • Canasta o bolsa de malla — mantiene los hallazgos intactos
  • Tu telefono (camara) — fotos del sombrero, abajo, tallo, base, habitat

Bueno Tener

  • Lupa 10x — examina laminas y poros de cerca
  • Libreta de campo — arboles cercanos, tipo de suelo, clima
  • Papel aluminio — envuelve ejemplares individualmente
  • Papel blanco + oscuro — para huellas de esporas
  • Toallitas humedas — lavate las manos despues de manejar hongos desconocidos
  • Donde crece? Suelo, madera, tronco, madera enterrada?
  • Que hay cerca? Roble, haya, conifera, bosque mixto?
  • Como crece? Solo, disperso, racimo denso?
  • Sombrero: Color, forma, tamano, textura?
  • Parte inferior: Laminas, poros, pliegues, espinas?
  • Tallo: Color, grosor, anillo, copa en la base?
  • Cortalo: Hueco? Solido? La carne cambia de color?
  • Olor: Afrutado? Terroso? Desagradable? Sin olor?
  • Fotos tomadas? Arriba, abajo, tallo, base, habitat — al menos 5
  • Huella de esporas? Papel blanco + oscuro, laminas abajo, 4–8 horas

Safety notice: This guide is for educational purposes. Never eat a wild mushroom based solely on a written guide. When in doubt, leave it alone. If you suspect poisoning, call emergency services immediately. Always consult an experienced forager before consuming wild mushrooms.
